Thermochemistry tracks energy crossing a chosen boundary. Identify the stated system first. In a reaction calorimetry problem it is usually the reacting chemicals. A closed system can exchange energy but keeps its matter. An open system can exchange matter and energy. An isolated system exchanges neither. The surroundings are outside the chosen boundary.

### Energy and signs
Kinetic energy comes from motion; potential energy depends on position or interactions. The mechanical energy of an object is its macroscopic kinetic plus potential energy. Internal energy describes microscopic contributions, including thermal motion and chemical interactions. Heat flows because of a temperature difference. Work is another mechanism for transferring energy, such as pushing a piston against external pressure. One joule is one newton-meter and one newton is one kilogram-meter per second squared.

| From the system’s perspective | Positive | Negative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heat, $q$ | Enters | Leaves |
| Work, $w$ | Done on the system | Done by the system |
| Volume change, $\Delta V$ | Expansion | Compression |

For pressure–volume work at constant external pressure, $w=-P_\mathrm{ext}\Delta V$. The first law is $\Delta E=q+w$. Heat and work depend on the path; internal energy and enthalpy depend on the state. For system plus surroundings treated as isolated, total energy change is zero.

### Enthalpy and amount
$H=E+PV$. At constant pressure with only pressure–volume work, $\Delta H=q_p$; at fixed volume with no other work, $\Delta E=q_v$. Exothermic means negative $q_p$ and endothermic means positive $q_p$. Enthalpy is extensive. The stated $\Delta H$ applies to the balanced reaction as written. Reverse the equation to reverse its sign; multiply every coefficient to scale $\Delta H$. Breaking a bond takes energy; forming a bond releases energy. The overall sign follows the balance of all changes.
