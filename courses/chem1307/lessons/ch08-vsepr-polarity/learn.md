Use the Lewis structure to count domains around one central atom. A single, double or triple bond to the same neighbor occupies **one** domain, and each central lone pair occupies one. Electron-domain geometry counts both bonds and lone pairs. Molecular shape names the arrangement of the bonded atoms. In $AX_mE_n$, A is the center, X counts bonded neighbors and E counts central lone pairs.

| Domains | Electron geometry | Molecular shapes as lone pairs increase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Linear, 180° | $AX_2$ linear |
| 3 | Trigonal planar, 120° | $AX_3$ trigonal planar; $AX_2E$ bent |
| 4 | Tetrahedral, 109.5° | $AX_4$ tetrahedral; $AX_3E$ trigonal pyramidal; $AX_2E_2$ bent |
| 5 | Trigonal bipyramidal, 90°/120°/180° | $AX_5$ trigonal bipyramidal; $AX_4E$ seesaw; $AX_3E_2$ T-shaped; $AX_2E_3$ linear |
| 6 | Octahedral, 90°/180° | $AX_6$ octahedral; $AX_5E$ square pyramidal; $AX_4E_2$ square planar; formal $AX_3E_3$ T-shaped, $AX_2E_4$ linear |

A lone pair usually takes more angular space than a bonding domain. On a tetrahedral arrangement this can compress remaining bond angles: methane is 109.5°, ammonia is about 107°, and water is about 104.5°. In five-domain geometries, lone pairs favor equatorial sites with fewer 90° interactions. In an octahedral arrangement, two lone pairs prefer opposite sites in the familiar square-planar case. The listed ideal angles come from the parent geometry, while actual angles can be distorted.

### From a bond dipole to the whole molecule
Electronegativity measures attraction for shared electrons. It generally increases across a main-group period and decreases down a group. The more electronegative partner is the partial-negative end. A larger electronegativity difference typically gives more bond polarity. For this course treat C–H as approximately nonpolar. Partial charges describe uneven electron density, while formal charges depend on the Lewis bookkeeping rule.

Place each bond dipole on the actual molecular geometry and take the vector sum. Symmetric arrangements with matching bonds can cancel, as in $\ce{CO2}$, $\ce{BF3}$, $\ce{CF4}$, $\ce{PCl5}$ and $\ce{SF6}$. A bent $\ce{SO2}$ molecule retains a net moment. A symmetric parent shape with different terminal atoms may also retain a net moment, as in tetrahedral $\ce{CH3Cl}$. A lone pair changes the shape and often the vector sum; square-planar $\ce{XeF4}$ still has cancelling dipoles.
