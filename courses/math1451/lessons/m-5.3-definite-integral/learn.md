The definite integral $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ is the limit of sums of the form $\sum f(x_i^*)\Delta x_i$ as the largest width shrinks to zero, when the limit exists independently of the sample choices. A continuous function on a closed, bounded interval is integrable. A bounded piecewise continuous function with finitely many jumps is also integrable. Changing the function at a single point does not change the integral.

### Read signs and units
A region above the axis adds to the integral. A region below subtracts. For total geometric area, split at sign changes and make all contributions positive, equivalent to $\int_a^b |f(x)|\,dx$. For velocity, $\int v$ gives displacement and $\int|v|$ gives distance. If $f$ is liters per minute and $x$ is minutes, the integral is in liters.

### Use the algebra of integrals
A reversed interval negates the result, equal bounds give zero, and splitting an interval at $c$ gives two integrals whose sum is the original. Pull constants outside and distribute across sums. If $m\le f\le M$, each rectangle lies between heights $m$ and $M$, so the integral lies between $m(b-a)$ and $M(b-a)$ for $a<b$. On $[-a,a]$, an odd integrand integrates to zero by cancellation, and an even one integrates to twice its integral over $[0,a]$.

### Read an integral from a sum
Identify the width first, then the interval and sampling point. For $\sum_{i=1}^n (4/n)\cos(2+4i/n)$, the interval starts at 2, has length 4, and the integrand is cosine. Its limit is $\int_2^6\cos x\,dx$.
