A single, double and triple covalent bond in a Lewis model have bond orders 1, 2 and 3. Bond length is the equilibrium internuclear separation. Within a comparable series of the **same bonded elements**, higher bond order generally means shorter and stronger bonding. Bond energies describe breaking bonds and are typically quoted for gas-phase species. A table’s average bond enthalpies represent similar bonds in different molecules, so they give approximate reaction enthalpies.

### Inventory the changes
1. Balance the reaction. Draw reactant and product connectivity and label all bond orders.
2. Multiply each molecule’s bond counts by its reaction coefficient.
3. Sum the enthalpies of all bonds broken in the reactants. These entries are positive.
4. Subtract the enthalpies of the bonds formed in the products. Formation contributes negatively because it is the reverse of dissociation.
5. Label the estimate in kJ per written reaction. Use specified formation data instead when a standard enthalpy for particular phases is required.

Identical bonds on both sides cancel in the average-energy model. You can count only changed bonds if you verify what remains identical. In a combustion problem, account for both C=O bonds per $\ce{CO2}$ and both O–H bonds per $\ce{H2O}$, along with the correct $\ce{O2}$ bond order. The gas-phase estimate does not include condensing the product water.
