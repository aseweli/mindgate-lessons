Electrons experience nuclear attraction and electron–electron repulsion. In the qualitative shielding model, $Z_\mathrm{eff}\approx Z-S$. Core electrons shield the outer shell effectively. Electrons in the same shell shield one another less effectively, so valence $Z_\mathrm{eff}$ generally rises across a period. Within a shell, greater penetration brings an electron closer to the nucleus on average; the usual order is $s>p>d>f$ in penetration. That helps explain why subshells of the same $n$ can have different energies.

### Radius and ionization energy
Atomic radii depend on the measurement convention. A covalent radius is half the internuclear distance in a homonuclear bond; a metallic radius is half the distance between nearest neighboring atoms in a metal. For main-group trends, atoms generally contract across a period and grow down a group as the outer shell has higher $n$. A cation usually shrinks relative to its atom and an anion usually grows. In an isoelectronic set, the species with more protons generally has the smaller radius.

First ionization energy refers to $\ce{X(g) -> X+(g) + e-}$. It generally rises across a period and falls down a group. Common exceptions: B has a lower first IE than Be because the removed electron is in 2p rather than 2s. O has a lower first IE than N because O has one paired 2p orbital and repulsion makes a paired electron easier to remove. Analogous comparisons occur for Mg/Al and P/S. Successive ionizations require progressively more energy. A large jump identifies the first core electron removed.

### Electron affinity
Use the course convention $\ce{X(g) + e- -> X^-(g)}$: a negative energy change means attachment releases energy. Many halogens have strongly negative values. Exceptions are substantial, so use configurations or supplied data rather than treating the trend as monotonic. For example, attachment to Cl is more exothermic than to F because the compact 2p shell of F increases electron repulsion. Some references instead report positive affinity for energy released, so read the sign convention in the question.
