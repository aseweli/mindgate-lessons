Continuity at $a$ requires three facts: $f(a)$ is defined, $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)$ exists, and the two are equal. At a closed interval endpoint use the limit from inside. At a piecewise join compute both limits separately and compare with the assigned value. A mismatch can be a removable hole, jump, or infinite discontinuity.

### Intermediate Value Theorem
If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$, it takes every value between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$. A sign change guarantees at least one zero in $(a,b)$. A sign change across a discontinuity gives no such guarantee. IVT proves existence; strict monotonicity or another argument is needed for uniqueness.

### Exponential and logarithmic functions
For $b>0$, $b\ne1$, $b^x$ is positive and continuous for all real $x$, and $\log_b x$ is its inverse on $x>0$. Base $b>1$ means increasing, while $0<b<1$ means decreasing. The exponent and log can undo one another where both expressions are defined. Use product and power identities with positive arguments. A natural log is base $e$; $\ln x\to-\infty$ as $x\to0^+$, and $e^x\to0$ as $x\to-\infty$.
