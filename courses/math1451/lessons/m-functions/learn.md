A function assigns one output to each input. Calculus adds two questions: how quickly is the output changing, and how much change accumulates? A secant gives average change and a tangent gives instantaneous change.

### Algebra and graphing
Check the original formula for restrictions: denominators stay nonzero, even roots require nonnegative radicands, and log arguments must be positive. Keep exclusions after simplification. In a composition $f(g(x))$, satisfy both the inner domain and the outer domain after substitution. Even functions reflect across the $y$-axis; odd functions have origin symmetry. $A f(B(x-h))+k$ shifts by $(h,k)$, scales vertically by $|A|$ and horizontally by $1/|B|$, and reflects when the corresponding coefficient is negative. Read intercepts, zeros, signs, and approximate extrema directly from a graph.

### Lines and parameters
Use point-slope form and keep vertical lines as $x=a$. For $x=x_0+ut$, $y=y_0+vt$, $(u,v)$ is the direction vector; eliminate $t$ to find the Cartesian relation and use the allowed $t$ values to get direction and endpoints. For example, $x=1+2t$, $y=5-t$ gives $y=(11-x)/2$; for $0\le t\le2$, the segment runs from $(1,5)$ to $(5,3)$.

### Radians and inverses
$\pi$ radians equals $180^\circ$. Read cosine and sine as the unit-circle coordinates; tangent is their quotient. In the first quadrant, sine at $0,\pi/6,\pi/4,\pi/3,\pi/2$ is $0,1/2,\sqrt2/2,\sqrt3/2,1$ and cosine reverses the list. Use the reference angle and quadrant signs. For an inverse, first require one-to-one behavior (horizontal line test), then switch input and output. Principal ranges are $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ for $\arcsin$, $[0,\pi]$ for $\arccos$, and $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ for $\arctan$. Restrict the domain if needed: $x^2$ on $x\ge0$ has inverse $\sqrt{x}$.
