Electromagnetic radiation consists of oscillating electric and magnetic fields perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel. Wavelength is crest-to-crest distance, amplitude measures the size of the field oscillation, and frequency counts cycles per second. In vacuum, all electromagnetic radiation travels at $c$. The product $\lambda\nu=c$ makes wavelength and frequency inversely related. One nanometer is $10^{-9}$ m, and $1\ \mathrm{Hz}=1\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$.

### Energy and the spectrum
From longest to shortest wavelength: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma. Frequency and photon energy increase along that sequence. Red visible light has a longer wavelength than violet. A photon carries $E=h\nu=hc/\lambda$. Keep “per photon” and “per mole of photons” separate. A molar energy equals energy per photon multiplied by Avogadro’s constant.

### Photoelectric effect
The threshold frequency $\nu_0$ supplies the work function $\Phi=h\nu_0$ of a metal. Below it, photons in the introductory one-photon model cannot eject electrons, even if the light is brighter. Above threshold, $KE_\mathrm{max}=h\nu-\Phi$. At fixed frequency above threshold, more intensity generally means more emitted electrons, while increasing frequency increases their maximum kinetic energy. Photon energy is determined by frequency; amplitude or beam intensity does not change each photon’s energy.
