Hess’s law works because $\Delta H$ depends on initial and final states. If a target reaction equals a sum of steps, the enthalpies add. Start by locating species unique to the target. Reverse and scale each step so those species land on the correct side with the correct coefficient. Add equations and cancel identical species with matching physical states before adding the modified enthalpies.

### Standard states
A standard enthalpy uses the reference states at the specified temperature. The usual table temperature is 298.15 K. Standard pressure is 1 bar. In introductory calculations use the course’s 1 mol/L reference for dissolved solutes. For an element, the formation reference is its stable form under those conditions, such as graphite for carbon or $\ce{O2(g)}$ for oxygen at 298.15 K. Its $\Delta H_f^\circ$ is zero by definition. An isolated gas-phase O atom or another allotrope need not have zero formation enthalpy.

### Formation reactions and the table method
A formation equation makes **one mole** of a substance from its constituent elements in their reference states. Fractions are appropriate. For $\ce{MgO(s)}$, use $\ce{Mg(s) + 1/2 O2(g) -> MgO(s)}$. When using a table, balance the target, include phases, multiply every $\Delta H_f^\circ$ by its coefficient, then subtract the entire reactant sum from the product sum. Using separate sums prevents losing a minus sign.
