A positive first derivative means the graph rises, while a negative one means it falls. A positive second derivative means slopes increase (concave up), and a negative one means slopes decrease (concave down). A graph can be decreasing and concave up at the same time, as $1/x$ is on $(0,\infty)$.

### Build two separate sign charts
1. Find the original domain and intercepts. Split at breaks even if simplified formulas hide them.
2. For the first chart, split at zeros and undefined points of $f'$. Test an input per interval. A $+\to-$ switch gives a local max, and $-\to+$ gives a local min at a continuous point. No switch means neither.
3. For the second chart, split at zeros and undefined points of $f''$. A concavity switch gives an inflection where the graph is continuous. Report $(c,f(c))$ if asked for the point.
4. Sketch using the charts, intercepts, and any asymptotes. A chart describes behavior on intervals. Mark the intervals using open endpoints at the breakpoints.

### Second derivative test
At a stationary point $c$ of a twice differentiable function, $f''(c)>0$ gives a local min and $f''(c)<0$ gives a local max. If $f''(c)=0$, use a sign chart. Compare $x^4$ and $-x^4$ at zero: both have zero second derivative there, yet they have opposite extremum types. $x^4$ also shows that a zero of $f''$ alone does not imply inflection.
