Write a configuration by counting electrons and occupying orbitals from lowest energy upward. Use the periodic table by blocks. The first two columns are s-block (helium is $1s^2$); the last six main-group columns are p-block; the transition metals are d-block; the two offset rows are f-block. A practical sequence is $1s,2s,2p,3s,3p,4s,3d,4p,5s,4d,5p,6s,4f,5d,6p,7s$. The preceding noble gas abbreviates the filled core. For main-group elements, the outer s and p electrons are valence electrons, and similar outer patterns explain group similarities. For transition atoms, occupied d electrons outside the noble-gas core can also participate in bonding.

### Occupancy and exceptions
Pauli forbids two electrons in an atom from sharing all four quantum numbers. A single orbital can hold two only with opposite spins. Hund’s rule puts one electron in each degenerate orbital with parallel spins before pairing; for p³, use three singly occupied boxes. For p⁴, use one pair and two singles. The common neutral exceptions tested here are Cr $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^5\,4s^1$ and Cu $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^{10}\,4s^1$. These configurations are observed; the simple filling order is a mnemonic and a complete energy explanation requires more than a half-filled-shell slogan.

### Ions and magnetic behavior
Add electrons for anions according to the usual filling pattern. For cations, start with the neutral atom and remove the highest-$n$ electrons first (when tied in these cases, remove higher $\ell$ first). For first-row transition-metal cations remove 4s before 3d. For example Fe is $[\mathrm{Ar}]3d^64s^2$; $\ce{Fe^2+}$ is $[\mathrm{Ar}]3d^6$. An isolated atom or ion with unpaired electrons is paramagnetic; one with all electrons paired is diamagnetic. Ferromagnetism is collective alignment in materials and requires interactions between moments. The presence of an unpaired electron by itself predicts paramagnetism for a simple isolated species, while bulk ordering is a separate question.
