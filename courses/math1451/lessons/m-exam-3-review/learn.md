Work on paper first: the answer field checks the result, while the written solution must show the reasoning.

| Wording or clue | First written step |
| --- | --- |
| Absolute extreme on a closed interval | List endpoints and interior critical numbers. |
| A tangent matching average rate | Verify MVT hypotheses and compute the secant slope. |
| Increasing, concavity, or sketch | Find the domain and make separate derivative sign charts. |
| Hole, asymptote or end behavior | Factor, keep exclusions, and take the relevant one-sided or end limits. |
| Indeterminate limit | State the form, then choose an algebraic rewrite or L’Hôpital. |
| Best dimension or least cost | Define an objective, constraint and feasible interval. |
| Recover a function from its rate | Integrate and apply the initial data. |
| Rectangles, sigma notation or definition | Write $\Delta x$, the sample rule and the sum. |
| Area or accumulated change | Decide whether the signs should cancel or be made positive. |

For a 70-minute practice session, spend the first 50 minutes working through every question you can set up. Spend the next 15 minutes returning to harder problems. Use the last five minutes to check endpoints, theorem hypotheses, signs, factors, and exact units.
