A derivative is the limit of a secant slope. Start with a symbolic increment $h$, expand, cancel the common $h$ while $h\ne0$, and then take $h\to0$. The tangent line uses the point $(a,f(a))$ and slope $f'(a)$. Differentiability implies continuity. A continuous graph can have a corner, cusp or vertical tangent where a finite derivative fails.

### Choose a rule from the outer operation
For sums, differentiate term by term. For products, differentiate each factor once and keep the other factor. For quotients, keep denominator squared and write the numerator in the order $f'g-fg'$. Simplifying first often saves algebra, but keep the original domain. For nonconstant inner expressions use the chain rule in the next lesson.

### Exact derivatives
$\sin\mapsto\cos$, $\cos\mapsto-\sin$, $\tan\mapsto\sec^2$, $\cot\mapsto-\csc^2$, $\sec\mapsto\sec\tan$, and $\csc\mapsto-\csc\cot$. Use radians. $e^x$ is unchanged and $a^x$ picks up $\ln a$. The derivative of $\ln x$ is $1/x$ for $x>0$; the derivative of $\ln|x|$ is $1/x$ for $x\ne0$. Rewrite radicals and reciprocals with powers when that simplifies the rule.
