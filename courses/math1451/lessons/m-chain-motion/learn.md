For position $s(t)$ on a line, velocity $v=s'$ carries a sign, speed is $|v|$, and acceleration $a=v'$ describes how velocity changes. Positive velocity means increasing position. Speed increases when $v$ and $a$ have the same nonzero sign. A zero of $v$ is a candidate for a reversal; test the sign on both sides. Average velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time. Total distance is the sum of absolute position changes on intervals of fixed direction.

For $f(g(x))$, differentiate the outside keeping the inside intact, then multiply by $g'(x)$. Repeat for every layer. Decide whether the overall expression is a product, quotient or composition before differentiating. For $e^{\sin(2x)}$, the three derivative factors are $e^{\sin(2x)}$, $\cos(2x)$ and $2$. For $\ln|u|$ the derivative is $u'/u$ on intervals where $u\ne0$. The inverse trig rules include $(\arcsin u)'=u'/\sqrt{1-u^2}$, $(\arccos u)'=-u'/\sqrt{1-u^2}$ and $(\arctan u)'=u'/(1+u^2)$.
