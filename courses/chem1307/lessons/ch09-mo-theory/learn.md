Molecular orbital theory places electrons in orbitals spread over a molecule. Two atomic orbitals combine into two molecular orbitals. Constructive overlap creates a lower-energy bonding MO with increased density between nuclei. Destructive overlap makes an antibonding MO with a node between nuclei and an asterisk. In the common convention with the bond along $z$, the two $p_z$ orbitals interact head-on to give $\sigma_{2p_z}$ and $\sigma^*_{2p_z}$. The corresponding $p_x$ and $p_y$ pairs give two degenerate $\pi$ bonding orbitals and two degenerate $\pi^*$ orbitals. Matching 1s or 2s orbitals yield $\sigma$ and $\sigma^*$ pairs.

### Choose the orbital order first
H and He have $\sigma_{1s}$ then $\sigma^*_{1s}$ as their only 1s MOs. For the second-row course diagrams, omit the filled core $\sigma_{1s}$ and $\sigma^*_{1s}$ when counting bond order because their equal populations cancel. Fill the valence MOs in this increasing energy order:

| Homonuclear diatomic family | Increasing valence MO energy |
| --- | --- |
| $\ce{Li2}$ through $\ce{N2}$ | $\sigma_{2s}<\sigma^*_{2s}<\pi_{2p}<\sigma_{2p}<\pi^*_{2p}<\sigma^*_{2p}$ |
| $\ce{O2}$ through $\ce{Ne2}$ | $\sigma_{2s}<\sigma^*_{2s}<\sigma_{2p}<\pi_{2p}<\pi^*_{2p}<\sigma^*_{2p}$ |

Each $\sigma$ orbital has two spin slots. Each degenerate $\pi$ pair has four slots in total, with one in each orbital before pairing. Count valence electrons from both atoms and adjust for charge. A molecular cation removes electrons from the highest-energy occupied MO (HOMO). A molecular anion places electrons in the lowest available MO level, pairing in a partially occupied degenerate set when appropriate. Use the parent molecule’s diagram order for these introductory ion comparisons.

### Read the occupied diagram
Bond order equals half of bonding electrons minus antibonding electrons. Removing an antibonding electron raises the predicted order by one half; adding one lowers it by one half. Change those signs if the electron is in a bonding orbital. A positive bond order suggests net bonding stabilization in this model. Zero means no net covalent bond is predicted by this simple calculation. An unpaired electron gives paramagnetism in this model; all paired gives diamagnetism. Inspect individual orbitals rather than assuming an even electron total guarantees pairing. For related species, a larger bond order generally means a shorter, stronger bond.
