A shell is identified by the first number in a label such as $3p$. Here $n=3$ and the letter $p$ means $\ell=1$. The subshell is the whole group of orbitals with that $n$ and $\ell$. Individual orbitals in it are distinguished by $m_\ell$. The spin label $m_s$ describes an electron in an orbital.

| Number | Allowed values | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $n$ | $1,2,3,\ldots$ | Shell, related to size and to energy |
| $\ell$ | $0$ through $n-1$ | Subshell and angular shape |
| $m_\ell$ | $-\ell,\ldots,+\ell$ | Orbital within a subshell |
| $m_s$ | $+\frac12,-\frac12$ | Spin projection of one electron |

The labels $s,p,d,f$ correspond to $\ell=0,1,2,3$. Thus $1p$ is impossible, $2p$ exists, and $2d$ is impossible. There are $2\ell+1$ orbitals per subshell and at most two electrons per orbital, giving capacities $2,6,10,14$. A shell has $n^2$ orbitals and capacity $2n^2$. In hydrogen-like species, orbitals with the same $n$ are degenerate in the introductory model. In multielectron atoms, penetration and shielding split subshell energies; Chapter 7 describes the filling order. The $n+\ell$ shortcut ranks 2p before 3s because both have 3 and the smaller $n$ wins. It is a filling mnemonic, while validity of a subshell comes from $\ell<n$.

### Shapes and nodes
The $s$ density is spherically symmetric. A real $p$ orbital has two lobes separated by an angular nodal plane. The phase signs on lobes are signs of $\psi$, while $|\psi|^2$ is nonnegative. Four familiar $d$ orbitals have four-lobed shapes; $d_{z^2}$ has lobes and a torus. A node is a surface where the wavefunction is zero. For hydrogen-like orbitals, total nodes are $n-1$, angular nodes are $\ell$, and radial nodes are $n-\ell-1$. Radial nodes are spherical surfaces; angular nodes can be planes or cones. On orbital-energy diagrams, vertical placement shows relative energy and equal height denotes degeneracy.
