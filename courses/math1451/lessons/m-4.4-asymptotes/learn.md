A **vertical asymptote** $x=a$ occurs when at least one one-sided limit at $a$ is infinite. A **horizontal asymptote** $y=L$ occurs when $f(x)\to L$ at one end of the real line. A **slant asymptote** $y=mx+b$ means the difference $f(x)-(mx+b)$ tends to zero. A graph may cross a horizontal or slant asymptote at a finite input.

### Rational-function workflow
Find exclusions in the original denominator, factor and simplify, then analyze the reduced formula near each exclusion. A canceled factor with a finite limit gives a hole. If a denominator factor remains and the numerator is nonzero there, check left and right signs for the vertical asymptote. Use original exclusions even if they vanish from the reduced expression.

At infinity, divide numerator and denominator by the largest denominator power or use leading terms. Smaller numerator degree gives 0, equal degrees give the leading-coefficient ratio, and larger numerator degree requires examining the growth and signs. If the numerator degree is one larger, polynomial division yields a slant line plus a remainder term that tends to zero.

### Radicals and separate ends
$\sqrt{x^2}=|x|$, so $\sqrt{4x^2+1}/x$ tends to 2 at $+\infty$ and $-2$ at $-\infty$. A radical difference can hide a finite limit: rationalize $\sqrt{x^2+x}-x$ at $+\infty$ to get $x/(\sqrt{x^2+x}+x)\to1/2$. Combine these limits with the derivative charts to keep branches on the correct side of each vertical asymptote.
